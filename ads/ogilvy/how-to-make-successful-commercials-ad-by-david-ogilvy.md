## Tekst (transkribert fra bildet)

**How to make successful television commercials**

by Ogilvy & Mather

Ogilvy & Mather has placed over $800,000,000 in television advertising.

We have created hundreds of successful television campaigns for our clients in the United States and abroad. We have spent $4,250,000 in research on the results of these campaigns.

Here is part of what we have learned.

PEOPLE don't watch television in order to see your commercial. Only one commercial in fifteen makes any impression at all on the viewer. Yet few packaged goods can survive without television. It is the most effective selling tool ever invented for the introduction of new products.

What follows are some ways you can get the most for the money you put behind television commercials.

**Your most important decision.** What you say is more important than how you say it. Your most important decision is *how to position your product*.

Should you position Dove as a product for older women with dry skin – or as a product which cannot irritate sensitive skin?

Should you position Shake 'n Bake as a new *kind* of chicken dinner – or as a better way to make fried chicken?

**Make a promise.** Once you determine how to position your product, boil your advertising down to a simple promise. Then deliver that promise as if your job depended on it. *Keep it simple.* Most television commercials are too complicated. They try to deliver too many ideas – and end up delivering none.

*Beware: If your promise can be construed to be in any way misleading, you run the risk of having to go to court. Caveat vendor.*

**What's the big idea?** It takes a big idea to jolt the consumer out of her indifference – to make her notice your commercial, remember it, and take action.

Big ideas are usually *simple* ideas – like the "Crown" campaign for Imperial Margarine, which ran for eleven years.

**Be consistent.** Too many good campaigns are yanked off the air before they have begun to pay off.

Your brand image should be consistent from year to year. Pepperidge Farm has promised old-fashioned goodness for fifteen years. Commercials for Nationwide Insurance have projected a consistent image for five years; awareness has *quintupled*.

[Bilde: Merrill Lynch is bullish on America, okser. Bildetekst: Unconventional visual idea. After just six weeks this campaign had increased awareness 65 percent.]

**Have a heart.** You are advertising to human beings. They cannot be bludgeoned into buying your product. A commercial that is warm and human is more likely to succeed than one which is cold and impersonal.

Ogilvy & Mather's introductory campaign for Spray 'n Vac rug cleaner delivered a strong promise in a human way. After six months of national advertising, Spray 'n Vac became the best selling brand in its category.

**The techniques that work best on television**

You must find a way to stand out from the 38,000 commercials the average viewer sees in a year. You will seldom break sales records unless you blaze new trails.

Ogilvy & Mather creative groups attempt to do unconventional commercials for every product they work on.

You would not expect to see a herd of bulls as the visual idea for a stockbroker's commercial. It worked for Merrill Lynch.

The television campaign for Mercedes-Benz is an unconventional automobile campaign. It is remarkably successful.

Here are some techniques that we have found work best, most often, on television. But to stand out they must be executed in an unconventional manner.

**Demonstrations.** Television permits you to *demonstrate* your product's benefits. Your words are important, but not as important as your pictures. Let your product do the talking.

**Testimonials.** In an era when people are growing more skeptical of commercials, testimonials by genuine, satisfied customers can be one of your most effective selling tools.

Ogilvy & Mather's testimonial commercials for the American Express Card helped increase Cardmembers forty percent faster than projections in three months.

**The continuing presenter.** Campaigns built around a "continuing presenter" – a character who appears in every commercial and becomes associated with your brand – often work for years.

Ogilvy & Mather has created a number of these remarkably effective sales people. Pete the Butcher sells Shake 'n Bake. Marion the Librarian endorses Ex-Lax. Cora sells Maxwell House Coffee in her General Store. And sales have never been better.

*The trick is to find somebody with an appropriate personality – and enough authority to speak for your product.*

But make sure that your product isn't overpowered by the personality of your presenter. You are asking people to buy your *product*, not your spokesman.

**The celebrity presenter.** People love to look at celebrities. Two years ago Ogilvy & Mather asked Joe DiMaggio to become the spokesman for The Bowery Savings Bank, the largest savings bank in New York.

DiMaggio is a New York hero. Awareness of The Bowery's advertising jumped 50 percent in eight months.

Before you use a famous person, be sure that he or she *likes* your product. If your presenter is not convinced, the camera will show it.

**"Slice of Life."** The "Slice of Life" works because it is based on the simplest form of teaching: the dialogue.

Questions from a Doubter are countered by persuasive answers from a Believer. Eventually, through dialogue, the Doubter is converted. This method of teaching worked for Plato. When handled skillfully, it can sell millions upon millions of dollars' worth of products today.

*Hint: Unless there is conflict between Doubter and Persuader, your "Slice" will fail. Dramatic conflict has been effective since Euripides. It works today for Lever Brothers' Aim, the most successful toothpaste introduction of the '70's.*

**Music.** Music is emotional shorthand. A quick way to put the viewer into the mood.

People remember lyrics more easily than spoken words. But the words of your song must deliver a *strong selling idea*.

If you have a single major promise, a song can help you deliver it. But we have found that it has a tendency to blur *secondary* copy points.

**Production: how to get the most for your money**

In the past ten years the cost of *producing* commercials has gone up 30 percent more than the cost of *placing* them.

So make sure that the commercial that you are producing is the right one. If you are testing several commercials, use the cheapest production technique consistent with giving each commercial a fair chance.

Once you have found the right commercial, don't rush pell-mell into production. Have your agency's producers estimate a price for the commercial. Then ask production houses to bid against that price. Compare the bids.

Some techniques are *always* expensive. If you use them, use them to full advantage.

When an animated character appears in a live-action situation, it costs plenty.

Hidden camera techniques are expensive. You may expose over 12,000 feet of film to use only 45 feet for a 30-second commercial.

Too many identifiable people in a commercial can cost you as much in residual payments as you spend on air time.

**How to test your commercials**

Good research can help you decide if you have a good commercial. Ogilvy & Mather has developed a research technique for measuring *when* attention drops, and *why*. We have another for measuring *communication* and *persuasiveness* in the same test.

Another technique measures campaign buildup or wear-out. This technique, the first of its kind, uses cable TV to measure *campaigns* rather than individual commercials. Your campaign is not a single exposure, so why should your research be based on a single exposure?

Research can help you find the answer. But it is not, in itself, the answer. Copywriters should keep their eye on *sales*, not on research scores.

**The commercial is only as good as the product**

The most powerful element of the commercial is the *product it sells*. People buy products, not television commercials.

In many successful campaigns the product sells itself. The agency simply presents it.

On the other hand, even the best commercial cannot sell a bad product beyond a first trial.

If you have a good product, follow the guidelines on this page. Chances are, your advertising will be as good as your product.

## Vurdering

- **Les:** delvis. Les "Your most important decision" til "Have a heart", og "Slice of Life"-avsnittet med hintet om konflikt. Skum produksjon og testing.
- **Oppsummering:** Ogilvy & Mather beskriver hva som virker på TV: posisjonering først, ett løfte, stor enkel idé, konsistens over år, varme. Så seks teknikker (demonstrasjon, testimonial, fast presenter, kjendis, slice of life, musikk), hver med et case og et tall. Avsluttes med råd om produksjonskostnad, testing og at produktet er viktigere enn reklamen.
- **Verdi:** ja. "Slice of life" beskrevet som Doubter mot Believer, med krav om konflikt, er nøyaktig malen for UGC-video som fungerer i 2026. Regelen om at presenteren ikke skal overskygge produktet er like relevant for creators.
- **Mekanisme:** eksempel på #7 (Big Idea), #31 (kampanje som løper i elleve år = truffet nerve), #61 (kjendisen credentialer: "be sure that he or she likes your product"), #57 (Star, Story, Solution: fast presenter som star), #44 (demonstrasjon som bevis). Kandidat til ny mekanisme: ja — Tvileren og troende (dialog med konflikt): salgsargumentet legges i munnen på en tviler som stiller innvendingene, og en troende som svarer, til tvileren snur. "Unless there is conflict between Doubter and Persuader, your 'Slice' will fail." Dette er en dramaturgisk struktur for video, ikke en copy-mekanisme, og dekkes ikke av #41 (paradigmeskifte hos leseren) eller #57 (format-kvalifisering). Innvendingene får en stemme i selve annonsen.
- **Stjel:**
  1. Skriv UGC-manus som dialog: en venninne som tviler ("bambus, er ikke det bare et moteord?") og en som har prøvd og svarer. Uten reell innvending faller det flatt.
  2. Testimonialen skal komme fra ekte, fornøyde kunder, og kjendisen/creatoren må faktisk bruke produktet. Kameraet avslører det motsatte.
  3. Test flere hooks billig før du produserer dyrt. Ogilvy: bruk billigste produksjon som gir hver variant en rettferdig sjanse.

## Reglene, kort

Annonsen har ikke nummererte punkter, men overskriftene fungerer som regler:

1. Viktigste avgjørelse: hvordan produktet posisjoneres. Hva du sier slår hvordan du sier det.
2. Kok ned til ett løfte og lever det. Ikke lov noe villedende.
3. Uten en stor, enkel idé legger ingen merke til reklamen.
4. Vær konsistent over år; kampanjer dør ofte før de har betalt seg.
5. Vær varm og menneskelig; ingen bankes til å kjøpe.
6. Demonstrasjon: la produktet snakke, bildene slår ordene.
7. Testimonials fra ekte, fornøyde kunder virker i en skeptisk tid.
8. En fast presenter kan bære merket i årevis, men må ikke overskygge produktet.
9. Kjendis bare hvis hen faktisk liker produktet; kameraet avslører det.
10. Slice of life virker fordi det er dialog, og den krever konflikt mellom tviler og troende.
11. Musikk er emosjonell stenografi, men sangen må bære salgsideen og kan viske ut sekundærpoeng.
12. Produksjon: test billig, finn den rette filmen, så hent inn anbud.
13. Test kampanjen, ikke bare enkeltfilmen, men hold øye med salg, ikke researchscore.
14. Reklamen er aldri bedre enn produktet.
