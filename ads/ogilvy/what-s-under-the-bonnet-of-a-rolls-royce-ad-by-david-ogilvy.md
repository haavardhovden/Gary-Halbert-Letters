## Tekst (transkribert fra bildet)

**Bildetekst (lang versjon):** "At 60 m.p.h. the loudest noise comes from the electric clock."

**Headline:** What's under the bonnet of a Rolls-Royce?

**Subhead:** An engine that has been run at full throttle for 7 hours before installation, acoustical mufflers that tune out sound frequencies, shock-absorbers that you can adjust from the driver's seat—these and a thousand other engineering refinements add up to a precision instrument that cruises gracefully at 90 m.p.h., costs $13,995, and never becomes obsolete.

*What makes Rolls-Royce the best car in the world? "There is really no magic about it—it is merely patient attention to detail," says an eminent Rolls-Royce engineer.*

Today, one of the most remarkable examples of this perfectionism can be found in the department at Rolls-Royce which makes the automatic gearboxes.

The brain-box, which selects the gears, is first bored to an accuracy of 1/4000th of an inch, which is normal at Rolls-Royce. It is then blasted by particles of ground *coconut* shell, under air pressure. The resulting mirror-smooth surface is then fine-bored with ground *oat-husks*. Ordinary mineral abrasives are never used—they would be too harsh.

**How they test your car**

Testing procedures at Rolls-Royce are fantastically thorough.

For example, every engine is run for eight hours on a test bed—thirty minutes idling, thirty minutes at normal revolutions, and *seven hours at full throttle*.

On removal from the test bed, one engine in twenty is stripped down to the last bolt. It is then laid out on a table and examined with microscopes.

If any component has faltered, every engine in the entire batch of twenty is taken out of production, stripped down, and then individually examined.

**Ninety-eight ordeals**

Your finished Rolls-Royce spends at least a week in the final test-shop being fine-tuned. Here it is subjected to ninety-eight separate ordeals.

If, for example, the gears on the rear axle are audible through a *stethoscope*, the entire rear axle is removed.

The finished car is test-driven over hundreds of miles of winding English roads, before delivery to you.

**Fourteen coats of paint**

The coachwork is given *five* coats of primer paint, and hand rubbed between each coat, before *nine* coats of color paint are applied and hand finished.

The radiator shells are made entirely by hand—in burnished stainless steel. Not a single surface on the radiator is flat. Its surfaces are *bowed* to an infinitely slight degree, on the same theory whereby the ancient Greeks made the outer edges of their temple columns slightly convex. In this way the design, viewed as a whole, appears geometrically correct.

This radiator has never changed, except that when Sir Henry Royce died in 1933, the famous monogram RR was changed from red to black.

**Seats in the House of Commons**

In upholstering the seats, Rolls-Royce uses the same English leather used on the seats in the House of Commons.

Eight full hides—enough to make 128 pairs of shoes—are selected from thirty or more to obtain perfect matching for color, texture, thickness and grain.

Once the hides have been selected for your car, a label containing your serial number is attached to each. The complete process of stitching, filling and covering the seats, squabs and door panels is then handled by one team of craftsmen.

**What's a Rolls-Royce like to drive?**

First and foremost, Rolls-Royce is the most *controllable* of cars. You can cruise at very high speeds with a feeling of sublime safety.

The power-assisted steering is responsive and *exact*—"the best in any car," reports ROAD & TRACK. You can really *feel* the road.

The driver sits slightly higher up than in ordinary cars. This gives you an exceptionally clear view of the road—including both front fenders.

**Stop squarely at 100 m.p.h.**

The power-assisted brakes are steady—you can apply them with full force at 100 m.p.h. and the car pulls up squarely, without any tendency to fade, or pull to one side. Only a very light pedal pressure is needed.

There are three independent systems of brakes on a Rolls-Royce—two hydraulic systems, and a mechanical system to the rear wheels, as well as mechanical hand braking. For absolute safety, the hydraulic brake systems are designed so that damage to one will not affect the other.

"If it had no other great qualities, the brakes of the Rolls-Royce would set it apart from cars of comparable size and performance built in any other country today," writes the Motoring Correspondent of THE OBSERVER. "A light pressure brings the powerful gearbox-driven servo into action and speed is simply *dissolved* away, silently and without a tremor."

You can drive long distances with amazing lack of fatigue. This is the result of many factors. Sensitive steering and smooth brakes. Good springing—not too soft, so you never get seasick. *By moving a switch on the steering column, you can adjust the shock absorbers to suit road conditions.*

Another switch defrosts the rear window by heating an invisible network of 1360 steel wires in the glass. There are two separate ventilating systems, and air-conditioning is available so that you can ride in comfort with all the windows closed.

**The loudest noise comes from the electric clock**

The silence of a Rolls-Royce comes from mechanical perfection. Three mufflers tune out sound frequencies—acoustically.

The silence in a Rolls-Royce is uncanny. "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise comes from the electric clock," reports the Technical Editor of THE MOTOR.

The car weighs 4780 pounds. But it is delicately maneuverable, and its great reserves of power are instantly available in an emergency. Acceleration is lively—a smooth, silent, inexorable surge that takes you from 0 to 60 m.p.h. in 11.6 seconds.

**You don't need a chauffeur**

This new Rolls-Royce is designed as an owner-driven car. It is eighteen inches shorter than the largest domestic cars. *It is easier to park.*

There is an element of simplicity about the appearance of the car which, says AUTOSPORT, "makes almost any other car seem cheap and tawdry by comparison." For example, the instruments on the dashboard. Instead of looking like a jukebox, they are austere white-on-black, with *legible* calibrations. And they are accurate. One instrument even tells you the level of oil in the crankcase.

There is a picnic table veneered in French walnut that slides out from under the dash. Two more swing out behind the front seats. The backrests on the front seats are individually adjustable.

There is a light for map reading under the dashboard, and individual lights beside each back seat which the occupants can use without disturbing the driver. There is a warning light on the dashboard which starts flashing when your gas tank gets down to its last three gallons.

You can lubricate the entire chassis while the car is in motion, by simply depressing a pedal from the driver's seat.

Every nut and bolt, every cotter pin and washer in your Rolls-Royce is chromium or cadmium plated to guard against corrosion. Even the tail pipe of the exhaust system is chromium plated.

The luggage compartment holds two two-suiter suitcases, three medium-size suitcases and two overnight bags—enough for a party of four on a drive from coast to coast.

You can also get an Espresso coffee-making machine, a dictating machine, a bed, hot and cold water for washing, an electric razor, and a telephone.

**Vettura di Signor Churchill?**

The psychological overtones of a Rolls-Royce are not to be denied.

"The unfeigned awe and admiration which the car excites everywhere is gratifying and sometimes moving," reports Gordon Wilkins in THE OBSERVER. "In Milan an old workman in faded blue overalls inspected my car with shining eyes, then removed his cap and said, 'Scusi, Signore. E la vettura di Signor Churchill?'"

And there is, above all, a profound satisfaction in handling such an exquisite piece of machinery.

**What about service?**

Some people hesitate to buy a Rolls-Royce because they have misgivings about service. Rolls-Royce service is a good deal better than the service on most ordinary cars. There is now a coast-to-coast network of dealers and parts-depots. Rolls-Royce headquarters in New York will fly spare parts to any point in the United States.

A man we know reports that a buzzard flew through his windshield in Texas recently—at 80 miles an hour. His Texas dealer had a new windshield flown in. Our man was back on the road the next day.

The Rolls-Royce people retain a fatherly interest in every car they make.

They make continuous engineering improvements, and these improvements are introduced as soon as they have been perfected, without waiting for a new model. Sometimes the improvements are introduced *retroactively*. In such cases the new part is sent to all dealers and installed in your car *without charge to you*.

**How expensive is a Rolls-Royce?**

The price of a new Rolls-Royce is $13,995. Before you decide that this is more than you can afford to invest, consider the following:

1. You can pay by installments over one, two or three years. We have heard of a bank in Chicago which allows you to spread payments over *six years*. And an increasing number of corporations are buying Rolls-Royce cars for their senior executives.

2. Annual depreciation is low and you will keep your Rolls-Royce for a long time—the average is eleven years.

3. A Rolls-Royce never becomes obsolete. You are assured of a *continuity of styling* that protects your investment. The styling of the Rolls-Royce, year after year, is always clean, graceful and functional—never outlandish.

4. Most new cars are guaranteed for 90 days only. Every Rolls-Royce engine and chassis is fully guaranteed for *three years*.

5. Most cars of today cost five or six times as much as they did twenty years ago. The new Rolls-Royce Silver Cloud costs the same as the Phantom III of twenty years ago. In terms of real dollars, the Rolls-Royce is an even better value than it used to be.

**What's the difference between Rolls-Royce and Bentley?**

The Bentley is made by Rolls-Royce. Except for the radiators, they are identical motor cars, manufactured by the same engineers in the same works.

*People who feel diffident about driving a Rolls-Royce can buy a Bentley.*

The Editor of THE AUTOCAR has this to say about the Bentley: "The latest Bentley model offers a degree of safety, comfort and performance that is beyond the experience and perhaps even the imagination of the majority of the world's motorists."

If you would like to try driving a Rolls-Royce or Bentley, get in touch with our dealer. You will find his name at the bottom of this page. Rolls-Royce Inc., 30 Rockefeller Plaza, New York 20, N. Y.

## Struktur

1. Oppmerksomhet: "What's under the bonnet of a Rolls-Royce?" pluss subhead med tre konkrete detaljer og pris.
2. Hvorfor bry seg: subhead: "a precision instrument that cruises gracefully at 90 m.p.h., costs $13,995, and never becomes obsolete."
3. Hvorfor tro: "There is really no magic about it—it is merely patient attention to detail," says an eminent Rolls-Royce engineer.
4. Bevis: kokosnøttskall og havreskall, "one engine in twenty is stripped down", "ninety-eight separate ordeals", stetoskop på bakakselen, sitater fra The Motor, The Observer, Road & Track, Autosport.
5. Fordeler: stille, kontrollerbar, stopper rett ved 100 mph, ingen sjåfør, lett å parkere, service med innflydde deler, gratis retroaktive forbedringer.
6. Hvordan bestille: "get in touch with our dealer. You will find his name at the bottom of this page."
7. Nå: "See the Rolls-Royce Silver Cloud at the Chicago International Trade Fair" (svak, kun i forhandlerversjonen).

Headline-type: spørsmål pluss kuriositet. Subheaden gjør løfte- og spesifisitetsjobben.
Bevissthetsgrad: produktbevisst. Skrevet for folk som kjenner merket og lurer på om det er verdt pengene.

## Vurdering

- **Les:** delvis. Les subhead, "How they test your car", "The loudest noise comes from the electric clock" og "How expensive is a Rolls-Royce?". Skum resten. Det er langversjonen av #19-annonsen, samme materiale i prosaform.
- **Oppsummering:** Annonsen er en helsides gjennomgang av hvordan Rolls-Royce bygges og testes: girkasse polert med kokosnøttskall, motorer kjørt sju timer på full gass, én av tjue plukket fra hverandre, 98 tester, 14 lag lakk, lær fra Underhuset, tre bremsesystemer. Den siterer fem motorblader, forklarer service og retroaktive oppgraderinger, gir en fempunkts prisbegrunnelse og forklarer forskjellen på Rolls og Bentley.
- **Verdi:** ja, men som referanseeksemplar, ikke som mal. Viser hvor langt du kan strekke "bevis gjennom prosess" når publikum allerede vil ha produktet og trenger begrunnelse.
- **Mekanisme:** #19 spesifisitet (dette er kildematerialet til #19 i prosaform), #38 flyt-verktøyene (mellomtitler som spørsmål, korte avsnitt, hver seksjon avsluttes i et sitat), #39 første blikk avgjør, lesingen er rettferdiggjøring (hele teksten er argumenter for et kjøp leseren allerede vil gjøre), #21 verdi-regnestykke (fempunktslista om pris). Kandidat til ny mekanisme: nei.
- **Stjel:**
  1. Beskriv kvalitetskontrollen, ikke kvaliteten. "Én av tjue motorer plukkes fra hverandre" slår "grundig testet". Stille: hva skjer faktisk med et sett sengetøy før det sendes? Hvis det er noe, si det med tall.
  2. Håndter prisen sist, med nummerert liste, etter at bevisene har gjort jobben. Nedbetaling, levetid, restverdi, garanti, sammenlignet med hva det kostet før.
  3. Én absurd, sann detalj per seksjon (kokosnøttskall, buzzard gjennom frontruta). Det er detaljene folk gjenforteller.
