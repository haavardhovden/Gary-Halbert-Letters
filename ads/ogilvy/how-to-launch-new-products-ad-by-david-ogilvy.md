# How to launch new products

Kilde: https://swiped.co/how-to-launch-new-products-ad-by-david-ogilvy · PDF: ads/ogilvy/src/how-to-launch-new-products-ad-by-david-ogilvy.pdf · Curriculum seksjon 2 (Ogilvy)

## Tekst (hovedpunkter, transkribert fra bildet)

**How to launch new products.** Ogilvy & Mather has helped to launch 73 new products. Forty-five are now established brands, double the usual rate of success.

**I. The Product.** Beware of launching a new product unless it has a real point of difference which can be perceived by the consumer. "Second" brands typically get only half the share of the pioneer. The difference may be better performance, quality, value, a new use, or solving a problem other brands do not solve. Too many new products have only minor technical differences which can only be perceived in a laboratory.

**II. The Package.** Package should help position the product and express its promise. Put your advertising promise right on the package, in the same words your advertising uses. Shake 'n Bake: "For crispy chicken without frying"; so does the advertising.

**III. The Name.** The name should help position your product and spell out its promise. Put the promise in the name (Shell No-Pest Strip, Roast 'n Boast). Test alternatives; test for negatives. Once launched under a name, that is its name forever.

**Direct Response: A Secret Weapon.** Pretest consumer appeal of a new product at a fraction of market test costs.

**IV. The Price.** Consistent with the image. Me-too product: never price above competition. Real difference: people will pay more.

**V. The Advertising.**
1. Results depend less on how it is written than how it is positioned. Positioning before advertising.
2. Unless built around a BIG IDEA, it will flop.
3. Your product will only be new once. Say it is new, loud and clear, and say what's new about it.
4. If possible, put the price in your advertising. It answers the first question every consumer is sure to ask.
5. Tell your customer how to use the product. Show the product in use.
6. Hammer home the name. Repeat it many times.
7. Don't be afraid to state the obvious. The benefits may be obvious to you. Not to the consumer.
8. Don't overclaim. Today's consumer is an intelligent and skeptical woman. If you make exaggerated claims, she will turn you off.

Stick with it: Pete the Butcher four years, Shake 'n Bake promise unchanged seven years, Dove "Face Test" eight years, Titus Moody fifteen years.

## Value og key takeaways

**Value:** Punkt 8. "Today's consumer is an intelligent and skeptical woman. If you make exaggerated claims, she will turn you off." Det er Jonathans feedback på overgangsalder-batchen, skrevet av Ogilvy i 1975.

1. Ikke overdriv. Hun er smart og skeptisk. Én for stor påstand, og hun er borte.
2. Si det åpenbare. Fordelen er åpenbar for deg, ikke for henne.
3. Løftet i samme ord overalt: pakke, navn, annonse. Ett løfte, samme ordlyd.
4. Sett prisen i annonsen. Det er det første hun lurer på.
5. Vis produktet i bruk. Fortell hvordan det passer inn i livet hennes.
6. Posisjonering før copy. Igjen. Han sier det i hver eneste annonse.
7. Hold på det. Samme løfte i sju år, samme presenter i femten.

Kort sagt: ett løfte, i hennes ord, uten å overdrive, og hold på det i årevis.

## Vurdering

- **Les:** delvis. Seksjon V, punkt 1–8.
- **Oppsummering:** Fem deler for lansering: produktet må ha en forskjell kunden kan se, pakken og navnet skal bære løftet, prisen skal matche bildet, og annonsen skal posisjoneres først, si at det er nytt, vise bruk, gjenta navnet, si det åpenbare og ikke overdrive.
- **Verdi:** ja, for punkt 7 og 8. Resten er repetisjon fra serien.
- **Mekanisme:** #7 Big Idea, #19 pris i annonsen, #31 hold på vinneren, #13-familien (ikke overdriv). Ingen ny.
- **Stjel:** "Say what's new about it" for hver ny Stille-farge eller -produkt: hva er nytt, sagt rett ut, én gang høyt.
